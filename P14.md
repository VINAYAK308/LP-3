# Survey Report: Types of Blockchains and Their Real-Time Use Cases

## 1. Introduction

Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology (DLT) that allows transactions and data to be recorded across a network of computers without requiring a single centralized authority to maintain the ledger.

A blockchain stores information in blocks that are cryptographically linked to previous blocks. Once data is recorded and sufficiently confirmed, altering it becomes difficult because the modification would have to be accepted by the network's consensus mechanism.

Blockchain technology was initially popularized by Bitcoin, but it is now used in areas such as finance, supply-chain management, healthcare, digital identity, gaming, and government services.

---

# 2. Types of Blockchains

Blockchains can broadly be classified into four types:

1. Public Blockchain
2. Private Blockchain
3. Consortium/Federated Blockchain
4. Hybrid Blockchain

---

## 2.1 Public Blockchain

A **public blockchain** is an open and permissionless blockchain. Anyone can generally join the network, participate in transactions, and verify blockchain data according to the network's rules.

### Characteristics

* Open to the public
* Decentralized
* No single organization controls the entire network
* High transparency
* Uses consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work or Proof of Stake
* Transactions can be verified by network participants

### Examples

* Bitcoin
* Ethereum
* Solana

### Real-Time Use Cases

**Cryptocurrency:**
Bitcoin allows users to transfer digital currency without requiring a traditional financial intermediary.

**Smart Contracts:**
Ethereum supports programmable smart contracts and decentralized applications (DApps).

**Decentralized Finance (DeFi):**
Public blockchains support decentralized exchanges, lending protocols, and other financial applications.

**NFTs:**
Blockchain can record ownership and transfer history for digital assets.

### Advantages

* High transparency
* Decentralization
* Strong resistance to censorship
* Global accessibility

### Disadvantages

* Scalability challenges on some networks
* Transaction fees can vary
* Public transaction data may create privacy concerns
* Consensus mechanisms can require significant resources depending on the blockchain

---

# 3. Private Blockchain

A **private blockchain** is a permissioned blockchain controlled by a specific organization. Only authorized participants can join the network or perform particular operations.

### Characteristics

* Permission-based access
* Controlled by an organization
* Faster transaction processing can be possible
* Greater privacy
* Participants are known to the network operator

### Examples

* Hyperledger Fabric
* R3 Corda
* Enterprise Ethereum implementations

### Real-Time Use Cases

**Banking:**
Banks can use permissioned blockchain networks for sharing transaction or settlement information between authorized institutions.

**Supply Chain:**
Companies can record movement of goods between manufacturers, warehouses, distributors, and retailers.

**Healthcare:**
Authorized healthcare organizations can share selected medical information while maintaining access controls.

**Enterprise Record Management:**
Organizations can maintain tamper-evident records shared between departments or business units.

### Advantages

* Better privacy
* High transaction throughput can be achieved
* Access can be controlled
* Suitable for enterprise environments

### Disadvantages

* Less decentralized
* Controlled by an organization or group
* Requires permission management
* Trust is partly placed in the network administrators

---

# 4. Consortium / Federated Blockchain

A **consortium blockchain** is controlled by a group of organizations rather than a single organization.

It is generally permissioned, with selected organizations participating in validating transactions.

### Characteristics

* Multiple organizations participate
* Permissioned access
* No single organization necessarily controls the entire network
* Suitable for business-to-business applications
* Consensus can be faster than in many public networks

### Real-Time Use Cases

### Banking and Financial Services

Multiple banks can share a blockchain network for:

* Interbank settlement
* Trade finance
* Payment processing
* Shared financial records

### Supply Chain

Manufacturers, logistics companies, suppliers, and retailers can share information about:

* Product movement
* Shipment status
* Product origin
* Delivery records

### Insurance

Insurance companies and other authorized participants can share claims information and records.

### Advantages

* Collaboration between organizations
* Better privacy than public blockchains
* Faster processing
* Reduced dependence on a single organization

### Disadvantages

* Requires agreement between participating organizations
* Governance can be complicated
* Membership is restricted

---

# 5. Hybrid Blockchain

A **hybrid blockchain** combines characteristics of public and private blockchains.

Some information or operations can be kept private while selected information can be exposed or verified publicly.

### Characteristics

* Combination of public and permissioned components
* Flexible access control
* Sensitive information can remain private
* Selected information can be publicly verifiable

### Real-Time Use Cases

**Healthcare:**
Private patient information can remain accessible only to authorized organizations while selected records or proofs can be verified externally.

**Government:**
Government organizations can maintain private records while publishing selected information for public verification.

**Supply Chain:**
Businesses can keep confidential commercial information private while allowing customers to verify product authenticity or origin.

**Real Estate:**
Private transaction details can be restricted while ownership records or proofs can be made independently verifiable.

### Advantages

* Flexibility
* Privacy for sensitive information
* Public verification where required
* Suitable for organizations requiring both transparency and confidentiality

### Disadvantages

* More complicated architecture
* Governance can be difficult
* Implementation can be more expensive

---

# 6. Comparison of Blockchain Types

| Feature           | Public                | Private             | Consortium                                | Hybrid                           |
| ----------------- | --------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Access            | Open                  | Restricted          | Restricted to participating organizations | Mixed                            |
| Control           | Decentralized         | Single organization | Multiple organizations                    | Combination                      |
| Transparency      | High                  | Limited             | Limited/controlled                        | Selective                        |
| Privacy           | Lower                 | High                | High                                      | High                             |
| Transaction Speed | Network-dependent     | Generally high      | Generally high                            | Generally high                   |

# 7. Real-Time Applications of Blockchain

## 7.1 Cryptocurrency

Blockchain provides the underlying infrastructure for cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin and Ether.

Transactions are recorded on the blockchain and validated according to the network's consensus rules.

**Examples:**

* Bitcoin
* Ethereum
* Litecoin

---

## 7.2 Banking and Finance

Blockchain can be used for:

* Cross-border payments
* Settlement
* Trade finance
* Digital assets
* Transaction records

It can reduce the number of intermediaries involved in certain processes and provide a shared transaction record.

---

## 7.3 Supply Chain Management

Blockchain can record the movement of products through different stages.

For example:

```text
Manufacturer
     ↓
Distributor
     ↓
Warehouse
     ↓
Retailer
     ↓
Customer
```

Each authorized participant can add relevant information to the shared record.

This can improve traceability and help organizations investigate where problems occurred in a supply chain.

---

## 7.4 Healthcare

Blockchain can be used to maintain controlled records of:

* Patient information
* Medical history
* Prescription records
* Insurance claims
* Medical research data

Access permissions can restrict who can view or modify associated information.

---

## 7.5 Digital Identity

Blockchain-based identity systems can provide a mechanism for users to manage or verify digital credentials.

Potential applications include:

* Educational certificates
* Professional credentials
* Identity verification
* Digital licenses

---

## 7.6 Voting Systems

Blockchain has been investigated for electronic voting systems.

A blockchain-based design could provide an auditable record of voting transactions while attempting to preserve voter privacy.

However, blockchain alone does not solve all electronic voting problems; identity verification, ballot secrecy, coercion resistance, endpoint security, and election administration remain important issues.

---

## 7.7 Real Estate

Blockchain can be used to maintain records associated with:

* Property ownership
* Property transfers
* Contracts
* Transaction history

Smart contracts can potentially automate parts of the transaction process.

---

## 7.8 Insurance

Blockchain can help organizations share claims information and automate parts of claims processing through smart contracts.

Potential applications include:

* Claim verification
* Fraud detection
* Policy management
* Automated settlements

---

## 7.9 Intellectual Property and Digital Assets

Blockchain can record ownership and transfer information for digital assets.

Applications include:

* Digital art
* NFTs
* Digital licenses
* Royalty tracking

---

## 7.10 Education

Educational institutions can issue digitally verifiable certificates.

For example:

```text
University
    ↓
Issues Certificate
    ↓
Blockchain Record
    ↓
Student
    ↓
Employer verifies certificate
```

This can make credential verification easier and reduce dependence on manually checking documents.

---

# 8. Blockchain and Smart Contracts

A **smart contract** is a program deployed on a blockchain that executes according to predefined conditions.

For example:

```text
IF payment is received
        ↓
THEN transfer digital asset
```

Ethereum is a major blockchain platform supporting smart contracts.

Smart contracts are used in:

* DeFi
* NFTs
* Decentralized applications
* Tokenization
* Automated agreements

---

# 9. Advantages of Blockchain

1. **Decentralization** – reduces dependence on a single authority in suitable network designs.
2. **Transparency** – transactions can be independently verified according to the network's access rules.
3. **Immutability/Tamper Evidence** – changing historical records can be difficult after confirmation.
4. **Traceability** – transaction history can be tracked.
5. **Automation** – smart contracts can automate predefined operations.
6. **Security** – cryptography and consensus mechanisms provide protection against unauthorized modifications.

---

# 10. Limitations of Blockchain

1. **Scalability** – some blockchain networks have limitations on transaction throughput.
2. **Transaction Costs** – public blockchain fees can fluctuate.
3. **Privacy** – public ledgers can expose transaction information.
4. **Regulatory Issues** – legal and regulatory requirements vary by application and jurisdiction.
5. **Energy Consumption** – some consensus mechanisms, particularly Proof of Work, can require substantial energy.
6. **Complexity** – blockchain systems require specialized technical and governance knowledge.
7. **Data Quality** – blockchain can preserve a record reliably, but it does not automatically guarantee that the original input data was accurate.

---

# 11. Conclusion

Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology with different architectures designed for different requirements. **Public blockchains** emphasize openness and decentralization, **private blockchains** provide controlled access for individual organizations, **consortium blockchains** enable multiple organizations to share a permissioned network, and **hybrid blockchains** combine controlled and publicly verifiable components.

Its applications extend beyond cryptocurrency into finance, supply chains, healthcare, identity management, education, insurance, digital assets, and other areas. The appropriate blockchain architecture depends on requirements such as **decentralization, privacy, scalability, governance, transparency, and access control**.
